# GrantOS: verified writing-resource cards
Verified 2026-10-11 through public official publisher pages. Links are public; no account or paid service was used. Source cards should be link-only with short original annotations, not a copied proposal library. Public access does not imply permission to reproduce, train on, or adapt application prose.

## Recommended six cards

### 1. NIH / NIAID: funded applications and reviewer feedback
- URL: https://beta.nih.gov/niaid/funding/sample-applications
- Evidence type: `funded_application`; badge: **Funded applications + reviewer feedback**
- Audiences: research; research-based small business.
- Provenance: NIAID publishes grantee-authorized application PDFs and summary statements. The collection includes R01 and other research mechanisms plus SBIR/STTR small-business samples. These are historical applications, not current forms.
- Concrete example: George Liu, UC San Diego, R01 application received April 10, 2023. Public 120-page application: https://beta.nih.gov/sites/default/files/migrated_media/2026-09/r01ai181321-01-liu-application.pdf . Contains aims, research strategy, budgets and supporting sections; some fields are redacted.
- Original lesson: Compare the aim, evidence, methods and feasibility argument with reviewer feedback. Separate what a section claims from the evidence supporting it.
- Rights: Application text is copyrighted. Stated permission is unchanged nonprofit educational use with investigator, institution and NIH/NIAID credit. Link only; do not import or adapt these texts into commercial drafting features. Respect redactions and omit contact/identity details from cards.
- Access note: Legacy niaid.nih.gov listing returned HTTP 405 to the research tool; the official beta.nih.gov listing and linked PDF were accessible.
- Current rubric companion: https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/peer-review/simplifying-review/framework (importance; rigor/feasibility; expertise/resources, for covered mechanisms). Match the actual opportunity, not historical sample headings.

### 2. NEA: funded arts-research narratives
- URL: https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/SampleNarratives-ORA-August2018.pdf
- Evidence type: `funded_narrative`; badge: **Funded narrative excerpts**
- Audiences: arts organizations; arts research; nonprofits.
- Provenance: NEA's August 3, 2018 release explicitly identifies project narratives from funded Research: Art Works proposals, including Teachers College, Columbia University (FY2016), Concordia University Chicago (FY2015) and George Mason University (FY2014).
- Original lesson: Explain the research gap, the exact question, the data or participants, the method and the intended contribution in a connected sequence.
- Caveat: These are project narrative sections, not complete applications. NEA explicitly says to treat them as references rather than templates, not to infer relative ranking, and to account for changing guidelines.
- Rights: FOIA/public release is not a blanket license for applicant-authored material. Link to the document; write original annotations rather than republishing prose.
- Current preparation companion: https://www.arts.gov/grants/grants-for-arts-projects/applicant-resources provides an application checklist/template, budget template and review-process tutorials. Label those **Official preparation tools**, not funded examples. Do not conflate GAP with the historical research program.

### 3. NJIT TAB: successful community-grant application documents
- URL: https://www.njit.edu/tab/sample-grant-applications
- Evidence type: `successful_application_collection`; badge: **Successful community application samples**
- Audiences: community organizations; nonprofits; local governments.
- Provenance: NJIT's Technical Assistance to Brownfield Communities program publishes a collection expressly titled Sample Successful Grant Applications, organized by FY2023–FY2025 and grant type.
- Concrete example: Safe Harbors of the Hudson, Inc., FY2025 community-wide assessment, requested $500,000. Public 17-page narrative/application document: https://www.njit.edu/tab/sites/njit.edu.tab/files/FY25%20Assessment%20CW%20-%20Safe%20Harbors%20of%20the%20Hudson,%20Inc.,%20NY.pdf
- Original lesson: Organize answers under the funder's exact numbered questions; connect place-specific evidence, community need, partners and planned work.
- Caveat: Successful provenance is the publisher's classification. This PDF contains narrative and supporting material; do not call it a complete electronic submission or infer reviewer scores. Historical environmental-grant requirements do not transfer to every community grant.
- Rights/privacy: The PDF says information may be made public, but no general adaptation license was verified. Link only; do not replicate contact details or signatures.

### 4. NYSCA / NYFA: artist fellowship recipients
- URL: https://www.nyfa.org/grant-history/nysca-nyfa-artist-fellowship/
- Evidence type: `awardee_directory`; badge: **Awardee directory, not applications**
- Audiences: individual artists.
- Provenance: NYFA's official grant-history directory names fellows, finalists and panelists by year and discipline.
- Original lesson: Research the range of supported artistic practices and choose work samples that clearly express your own artistic voice. An awardee's public portfolio can offer context but is not their submitted application.
- Caveat: Recipient records do not reveal application wording, panel scores or why an artist was selected. Never advertise these as winning proposals.
- Review companion: https://www.nyfa.org/awards-grants/nysca-nyfa-artist-fellowship/ says selection is based on artistic vision and voice through peer-review rounds; it is an unrestricted fellowship, not a project grant. Check the relevant year's discipline instructions.
- Rights: Link to profiles; no artwork, photographs or prose reproduction permission verified.

### 5. NSF: funded project abstracts and business awards
- URL: https://www.nsf.gov/funding/award-search
- Evidence type: `funded_abstract`; badge: **Funded project abstracts, not proposals**
- Audiences: research; technology businesses.
- Provenance: Official searchable award database, including award abstracts; small-business browse entry: https://seedfund.nsf.gov/awardees/history/
- Original lesson: Study concise explanations of the technical problem, proposed innovation and potential public benefit. For a business application, support the market opportunity and technical advantage with your own evidence.
- Caveat: NSF explicitly says Award Search supplies abstracts only; proposals remain submitting organizations' confidential intellectual property. No success probability can be inferred from textual similarity.
- Rights: NSF states award data, including abstracts, is public domain. Linked papers and proceedings may have separate copyright. Keep GrantOS link-first regardless.
- Rubric companions: https://www.nsf.gov/funding/merit-review (intellectual merit and broader impacts); https://seedfund.nsf.gov/resources/review/merit-review/ (adds commercial impact for small business). The actual solicitation remains controlling.

### 6. Candid: nonprofit proposal structures and samples
- URL: https://candid.org/blogs/four-common-examples-grant-proposal-documents-free-samples-included/
- Evidence type: `writing_guidance`; badge: **Writing guide + sample documents**
- Audiences: nonprofits; foundation applicants.
- Provenance: Candid's own July 13, 2023 guide covers letters of inquiry, cover letters, budgets and full proposals, with sample-resource links.
- Original lesson: Keep the need, proposed activities, organizational capacity and funding request consistent. Budget totals should reconcile with the narrative's requested amount.
- Caveat: The guide describes successful examples, but this pass did not verify award provenance for each linked sample. Do not label the guide or all its samples as verified funded applications. Candid is a grantseeker-support organization, not the funding body or a universal reviewer rubric.
- Rights/access: Public guide is accessible without login; individual sample destinations may differ. Candid reserves rights. Link rather than mirror.

## Optional local arts/community substitute
LMCC 2025 Manhattan Arts Grants recipients: https://lmcc.net/announcing-2025-manhattan-arts-grants-recipients/ . Label `awardee_directory`, **Awarded projects / recipients, not applications**. Official announcement verifies awards to artists and organizations; it does not publish submitted narrative text. Useful for understanding local arts/community project scope. Current program pages have changed, so do not reuse historic amounts or criteria as current eligibility facts. https://lmcc.net/resources/manhattan-arts-grants/creative-unrestricted/ describes criterion-based panel review and explicitly says past support does not guarantee future support.

## Implementation contract
Suggested fields: id, title, url, publisher, audience[], evidenceType, badge, verifiedAt, period, provenance, learnFrom, limitations, rightsNote, companionLinks[]. Set verifiedAt to 2026-10-11. No fabricated funding rate, score, panel endorsement, number of winning applications reviewed or amount awarded beyond source evidence.

Use the panel title **Learn from funded work and official guidance**. A clear global note: “Examples show structure and context, not a formula for funding. Follow the current funder instructions and write from your own evidence.” Never use a generic 'winning proposal' badge for directories, abstracts, sample templates or the application's generated starter text.

Coach can offer original assistance with: answering the actual question, specificity, evidence-needed flags, measurable outcomes, feasible timeline, budget consistency, work-sample alignment and readable structure. Call these suggestions or completeness checks, not funder scores. Preserve first-person voice, unknowns and user facts; never invent impact counts, partners, qualifications, quotations, budgets or commitments. Any generated example must be labeled **Illustrative starter, not a funded application**. Do not assert that a heuristic score predicts award likelihood.
